## THE PILOT'S STANDING RULES — these are not negotiable

* **Ask before writing or fitting any guard.** "koi bhi guard likhne ya
  lagne se pahle pucho". A guard is anything that rejects, clamps, vetoes
  or zeroes. Measurements, logging and corrections to geometry are not
  guards; thresholds are.
* **Nothing may be gated on GNSS.** "vps ke time par kuch bhi ho jaye GPS
  ka data nahi aana chiye". If VPS data stops the answer is ALT_HOLD, never
  an automatic fall back to GNSS.
* **Switch means switch.** When the pilot flips to GPS, the Jetson stops
  injecting immediately and completely.
* Radio/pilot-facing text is **English and professional**. Chat replies are
  in **Hinglish**.
* **When asked a question, answer it — do not act.** "jab pucha jaye tho
  srif bato kuch caro nahi".
* Commit and **push to BOTH branches** after every change. The pilot test
  flies and does not want work lost.
* Weight recent flight evidence over archived reports.

## HOW TO MEASURE, OR YOU WILL BE WRONG

`memory/dronevps-measure-fairly.md` lists the ways this project has drawn
false conclusions. The ones that keep biting:

1. **Score position against `gps1_north_m`/`gps1_east_m`** — GPS_RAW_INT,
   the only independent truth. **Never** against `gps_north_m`, which is
   GLOBAL_POSITION_INT, the EKF's fused output, which on VPS is our own
   injection coming back. Better still, take lat/lon from the FC .BIN and
   anchor the clock on the "SOURCE -> VPS" message, then sanity-check that
   the first seconds after handover read near zero.
2. **The `gps1_*` columns contain corrupt rows** — up to 23 km. Filter to
   within ~400 m of the origin before using them.
3. **Smooth both sides equally.** Comparing raw flow against a 1 s GPS
   difference produced a fake "2.22x scale error" that evaporated at equal
   smoothing (0.79-1.02).
4. **Replay before blaming a code change.** Flight 160's matcher collapse
   looked like my yaw offset; replaying that flight's own frames with the
   change on and off cleared it in one command.

## 26 October — the lidar's zero, and why it is not the scale

The pilot reported drifting and wobble below 60 m on flights 246 and 247, and
the rangefinder read **6.94 m with the lens 0.26 m above the floor**. Two
things came out of chasing that, and the second one matters more.

**The SCALE is right. Do not change `RNGFND1_SCALING`.** It is 72.61 m/V, so
one millivolt is 7.3 cm. Asked to try 65 and then lower, measured on the
aircraft with the lens at 0.26 m:

    SCALING   72.61 -> 7.02 m     50.0 -> 4.38 m
              65.0  -> 5.70 m     30.0 -> 2.60 m
                                  10.0 -> 0.86 m

Reaching 0.26 m needs SCALING about 3, which would cap the sensor at 7 m of
range. Scaling is the SLOPE - it shrinks everything, not the zero. Two
independent measurements already defend 72.61: the 20 Oct hover-minus-ground
test gave 4.47 m against the EKF's 4.45, and on log 246 against the GPS
altitude the lidar fits `0.898 * GPSalt - 0.04 m` at corr 0.980 - it passes
through zero.

**The ZERO is what moves, and it moves between sessions.** The pin sat at
0.768 V when logs 246/247 were flown and at 0.855 V today - 87 mV, which at
7.3 cm per millivolt is 6.3 m. `RNGFND1_OFFSET` was re-zeroed to **0.85450**
and the reading went 6.94 m -> 0.298 m against a true 0.26.

**The barometer really is high, confirmed independently.** On log 246 against
the GPS altitude:

    lidar = 0.898 * GPSalt - 0.04 m    corr 0.980
    baro  = 0.889 * GPSalt + 5.49 m    corr 0.989

Same slope, and the slope is the GPS's own vertical error, not theirs. The
OFFSETS are the finding: the lidar is at zero, the barometer is +5.5 m. That
is the premise 3726bbf rests on and it holds.

**FIELD PROCEDURE: none. The node does it.**

A three-command procedure was written here first - zero the lidar, restart the
node, check the CSV - and the pilot rejected it on sight: *"ground mein ja kar
ye sab mai kaise kar sakta hu, possible nahi hai aur har baar ye karna thik
bhi nahi hai."* They are right, and the objection is a design one rather than
a convenience one. A parameter that has to be rewritten before every flight is
a worse thing to own than a number computed from the data we already have.

So `RNGFND1_OFFSET` on the flight controller is **left alone** and the node
measures the zero itself: `VpsNode._rngfnd_zero`, a rolling median of the
rangefinder taken only while **disarmed and within 1.5 m of the ground**,
minus the 0.26 m standing clearance. It keeps tracking until the moment of
arming and freezes there, because the zero wanders both ways and the value
that matters is the one at lift-off.

Sitting disarmed on the ground also clears `_alt_bias_m` to 0. That fixes a
real trap: `ALT_BIAS_LIDAR_MIN_M` is 2.0, so below 2 m the barometric bias
stops updating but **keeps its last value**, and the node was found holding
-5.81 m and reporting 5.5 m of altitude while the aircraft sat on the floor.
Nothing but a restart used to clear it.

`rngfnd_zero_m` is a CSV column, so what the aircraft lifted off with is in
the log. `tests/test_rngfnd_zero.py` locks the tracking, the freeze at arming,
the altitude gate, and the stale-bias clear.

The FC's own `RNGFND1_OFFSET` was still set to 0.85450 on 26 Oct, because
ArduPilot's land detector reads the rangefinder directly and a zero of several
metres is what stopped the aircraft disarming on 20 Oct. That is the only
reason it is kept near-right; our own height path no longer depends on it.

**Still open on the lidar:** the zero wandered 0.298 -> 0.391 m in 65 s today
(2-4 m/hour against the 0.65 m/hour recorded on 21 Oct), and the pilot saw it
settle downward to 0.1-0.2 m over a few minutes. It wanders both ways, so it
is noise and not a one-way drift. The size is about 2 mV of ADC, which is a
CONSTANT metre error at every altitude rather than a proportional one - 0.15 m
at 1 m and 0.15 m at 100 m - so it is harmless for measuring a 5-7 m
barometric offset and must not be allowed to drive height directly.
`EK3_RNG_USE_HGT` stays -1.

## 26 October — why the map dies below 60 m, and it is not the window

The pilot has lowered `map_min_alt_m` before and reported it made things
worse. Measured now on the 25 Oct flights' own saved frames (2886 + 1601),
276 frames at 28-62 m, four window floors:

    window floor      window at 40 m      accepted
    40 m (as flown)         80 m           0/276
    25 m                    50 m           0/276
    15 m                    48 m           0/276
    none (0.60*alt)         48 m           0/276

**Zero in all four.** The window is not the fault and no setting of it is.
(An earlier entry today called the 40 m floor "the direct answer to B2". It
is not; that was an arithmetic argument and this is the measurement.)

The fault is that **the query runs out of pixels**. `north_up_query` rescales
the camera frame DOWN to the map's 0.509 m/px, so the query's size in pixels
falls with altitude, and XFeat's match count falls with it:

    alt   footprint      query        XFeat raw matches
    30 m   48 x 30 m     56 x 56 px            9
    38 m   61 x 38 m     71 x 71 px            9
    52 m   84 x 52 m     97 x 97 px           17
    80 m  129 x 80 m   150 x 150 px          145
    94 m  151 x 95 m   176 x 176 px          165

`min_inliers` is 12, so at 30-38 m it fails on "only 9 matches" before inliers
are counted at all. At 80 m and above the same code accepts 3 of 5 frames.

**This is the map's resolution, not a tuning fault.** At 30 m the camera sees
48 m of ground, and 48 m of this sheet is 95 pixels. There is not enough map
to describe the patch, whatever the window or the gates do.

**It also puts one item back on the table, for low altitude only.** "A finer
map gives nothing" was measured ABOVE 60 m and stands there - the map's own
0.509 m/px gives 9.0 m against the current 8.9 m. Below 60 m the binding
constraint is map pixels per footprint, which is exactly what a finer map
fixes: at 30 m a 0.10 m/px sheet would give a 480 x 300 px query instead of
56 x 56. Whether that is worth the imagery is the pilot's call, and it is the
only route to map fixes during the descent that this measurement leaves open.

Upscaling the existing map is NOT that route and is already on the do-not-retry
list - raw matches 124 -> 241 but inliers 8 -> 1. Interpolation is not detail.

## 26 October — the lidar's noise is a setting, and it is set wrong

The pilot asked whether the 150 Ohm resistor could be heating and shifting the
reading. It is not the resistor, and the pilot's own two-distance test is what
settled it: with the lens 0.26 m above the floor the error was **+0.24 m**, and
held level with a 77 cm table (1.03 m to the floor) it was **+0.20 to +0.30 m**.
A resistor fault scales with distance - at 1.03 m it would have been about
+0.95 m and read 2.0. A constant error at both distances is a ZERO offset.

Ruled out by measurement the same evening: the board rail (Vcc mean 5.0933 V,
sd 0.0058, correlation with the reading **-0.110**, so the rail's own movement
explains 0.011 m of it) and self-heating (the resistor dissipates 5.4 mW).

**What the manual says, and it is the fix.** The TF03's analog span is a
SETTING, not a property. Section 4.1: "The default value of maximum range is
180m, it can be set with Over range threshold", and Table 7 carries a separate
"Set the upper limit distance of analog signal". It ships at 180 m.

Sixteen milliamps across 180 m through 150 Ohms is 2.4 V across 180 m, so
**one millivolt is 7.26 cm** and every millivolt of drift anywhere in that
chain is seven centimetres of reported height. That is the whole fault.

    span 180 m   1 mV = 7.26 cm   RNGFND1_SCALING 72.61   <- what has flown
    span  60 m   1 mV = 2.50 cm   RNGFND1_SCALING 25.00
    span  30 m   1 mV = 1.25 cm   RNGFND1_SCALING 12.50

`ALT_BIAS_LIDAR_MAX_M` is 50, so this aircraft never needs the lidar above
50 m. The 180 m it ships with is 130 m of nothing bought at a 3x noise penalty.
At 60 m the +0.25 m offset becomes 0.086 m and today's 4.5 m/hour of wander
becomes 1.5.

**How, and why it is not automatic.** "The two interfaces, Analog Signal and
UART, cannot work simultaneously" - the UART on this variant is for
configuration only. It needs a USB-TTL adapter on the TF03's own UART,
115200 8N1, once, on the bench. The analog wiring is untouched.

    python3 vps_vio/tf03_range.py --port /dev/ttyUSB0 --span-m 60 --apply
    then RNGFND1_SCALING 72.61 -> 25.00
    then python3 vps_vio/lidar_zero.py --true-m 0.26 --apply

The span and the scaling are two halves of one number; change one without the
other and the aircraft reads nonsense. `tests/test_tf03_range.py` checks the
frames against the four the manual prints in full, because Len is the length
of the WHOLE frame and not of the payload - get that wrong and the sensor
silently ignores the command, which on a bench looks like bad wiring.

**Also measured, and it is why the reading died today.** `RNGFND1_OFFSET` was
set to 0.8545 V after hours of upward drift; the power cycle brought the pin
back down below it, ArduPilot floored the negative distance at zero, and
DISTANCE_SENSOR read 0.00 with the sensor perfectly healthy. The zero must be
taken AFTER power-on, never carried across one. `lidar_zero.py` detects the
clamp and walks the offset down; it landed on 0.75349 V and 0.316 m.
