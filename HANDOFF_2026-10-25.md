## THE PILOT'S STANDING RULES — these are not negotiable

* **Ask before writing or fitting any guard.** "koi bhi guard likhne ya
  lagne se pahle pucho". A guard is anything that rejects, clamps, vetoes
  or zeroes. Measurements, logging and corrections to geometry are not
  guards; thresholds are.
* **Nothing may be gated on GNSS.** "vps ke time par kuch bhi ho jaye GPS
  ka data nahi aana chiye". If VPS data stops the answer is ALT_HOLD, never
  an automatic fall back to GNSS.
* **Switch means switch.** When the pilot flips to GPS, the Jetson stops
  injecting immediately and completely.
* Radio/pilot-facing text is **English and professional**. Chat replies are
  in **Hinglish**.
* **When asked a question, answer it — do not act.** "jab pucha jaye tho
  srif bato kuch caro nahi".
* Commit and **push to BOTH branches** after every change. The pilot test
  flies and does not want work lost.
* Weight recent flight evidence over archived reports.

## HOW TO MEASURE, OR YOU WILL BE WRONG

`memory/dronevps-measure-fairly.md` lists the ways this project has drawn
false conclusions. The ones that keep biting:

1. **Score position against `gps1_north_m`/`gps1_east_m`** — GPS_RAW_INT,
   the only independent truth. **Never** against `gps_north_m`, which is
   GLOBAL_POSITION_INT, the EKF's fused output, which on VPS is our own
   injection coming back. Better still, take lat/lon from the FC .BIN and
   anchor the clock on the "SOURCE -> VPS" message, then sanity-check that
   the first seconds after handover read near zero.
2. **The `gps1_*` columns contain corrupt rows** — up to 23 km. Filter to
   within ~400 m of the origin before using them.
3. **Smooth both sides equally.** Comparing raw flow against a 1 s GPS
   difference produced a fake "2.22x scale error" that evaporated at equal
   smoothing (0.79-1.02).
4. **Replay before blaming a code change.** Flight 160's matcher collapse
   looked like my yaw offset; replaying that flight's own frames with the
   change on and off cleared it in one command.

## WHAT THE SYSTEM IS NOW

Flow from two OAK cameras -> per-track derotation from the camera gyro ->
`shift_to_ne` -> a 40-frame velocity baseline -> `VelocityBridge` (blends
camera acceleration, tau 0.35 s) -> deadband 0.35 + 0.010*alt -> 6 m/s cap
-> GPS_INPUT as GPS2. Position is origin + integrated flow, corrected by a
map matcher above 60 m.

Key settings as they stand (`vps_vio/config.py`, `map_fuse.py`):

    matcher                 "both"  (NCC and XFeat every attempt)
    camera_yaw_offset_deg   -1.95
    flow_vel_frames         40
    fix_period_s            2.0
    fix_sigma_m             20.0
    max_correction_m        0.5
    max_fix_speed_mps       5.0
    deadband                0.35 + 0.010 * altitude

## WHAT FLIGHT 245 STILL SHOWED, AND IS STILL OPEN

1. **The aircraft jerks.** The sent speed crosses zero **307 times in
   634 s** and the deadband zeroes 39.9% of rows. It is a hard threshold -
   below it the FC is told exactly 0, above it the full value - so every
   crossing is a step. Commanded tilt saturates at ATC_ANGLE_MAX 15 deg,
   rates to 56.9 deg/s. Map fixes are ruled out (16% coincidence against a
   15% base rate).
2. **Toilet bowl at 35 m.** The bearing from its own centre swept 754
   degrees in 24 s while yaw moved 9.6 - circling, not turning. Period
   11.4 s; 2*pi times the 40-frame baseline's 1.6 s lag is 10.1 s.
3. **The barometer is 5-7 m high.** Against the lidar: ratio 1.07 at 100 m,
   1.17 at 40, 1.29 at 20, **1.72 at 10**. The same climbing and descending,
   so an offset and not hysteresis. At touchdown the lidar read 0.60 m and
   alt_m read 4.2. Velocity is pixels*altitude/focal, so that ratio is the
   velocity error - 72% at 10 m.
4. **Error creeps** from 1.4 m to ~10 m at 82 m over a few minutes.

## THE PILOT'S OWN DIAGNOSIS, WHICH HAS BEEN RIGHT REPEATEDLY

* The map correction being applied to a position the aircraft had already
  left — **correct**, the prior was never used. Fixed in c106c6e.
* The matcher agreeing on grass rather than building corners — **correct**,
  9% of inliers sit on strong edges against 25% for random placement.
* The camera being rotated on the airframe — **correct**, -1.95 deg on two
  flights.
* Tilt moving the matcher's answer — **correct**, and the biggest single
  improvement to the map (11.18 -> 8.69 m).
* The FC and camera swinging in **opposite** directions when the nose
  pitches down — **correct**; the FC is 8 cm above the CG and the camera
  22 cm below. I had it inverted first.
* The barometer reading high at low altitude — **correct**, measured above.

Take their physical intuition seriously and measure it rather than
arguing.
