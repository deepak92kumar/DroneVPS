## 2 — Two parameter files were built for the new board

**`reports/new_fc_setup.parm`** — 892 parameters, the complete configuration.
Built from the flight 238 snapshot (a flight that took no failsafe) with every
measured change since folded in and marked `# MEASURED`. 194 parameters are
deliberately **absent**: all calibration, every device id, the counters and the
learned hover throttle. Those describe the old board's sensors, and copying
them onto a new board hides whether it was ever calibrated.

Four lines carry `# CHECK` — `BATT_VOLT_PIN`, `BATT_CURR_PIN`,
`BATT_VOLT_MULT`, `BATT_AMP_PERVLT`. The X7+ may not number its analog pins
the way the X7 did. A wrong pin gives nonsense battery readings, and then
`BATT_ARM_VOLT 23.3` refuses to arm.

**`reports/vps_only.parm`** — 41 parameters, only what the VPS needs, in eight
groups with the reason on each line. Use this when the aircraft already flies
and only the VPS has to be brought up.

Both are regenerated by `fc_scripts/build_newfc_parm.py` and
`fc_scripts/build_vps_parm.py`.

### Order matters

Load the file **first**, calibrate **after**. `FRAME_CLASS` and the SERIAL
ports have to be right before anything else means anything. Then:

1. accelerometer calibration (6 point)
2. compass calibration — then decide `COMPASS_USE2` from what it finds
3. RC calibration — **RC6 must end at MIN 1045 / MAX 1945** or the GPS/VPS
   switch stops meaning anything
4. check the four `BATT_` lines against the X7+ pinout
5. `python3 /data/DRONE/vps_vio/preflight.py`

## 3 — The crash, 31 Oct, log 00000024

The board rail collapsed in flight and the aircraft came down.

```
Vcc sat at 5.140 V, flat, for 157 seconds
    t = 157.5 s     5.140  ->  4.595      one sample, -545 mV
    then            4.55, 4.54, 4.46 ...  never recovered
    min             4.452 V
    log ends        179 s
```

It is not a current shortage. Across that step the battery moved 0.24 V and
the motors stayed at 1762-1768. A load problem sags with the motors; this
jumped in one sample and then sat flat.

**This is the second time and it is the same signature as 27 Oct:**

```
27 Oct  log 259    5.071 -> 4.162   one sample, aircraft fell
31 Oct  log 024    5.140 -> 4.595   one sample, aircraft fell
```

Every flight in between (252, 255-258, 271, 11-14) held 5.011-5.115 with no
dip at all. It is an intermittent connection, not a worn-out supply.

The `GPS Glitch` at 167 s and `Internal Errors 0x4000` at 176 s are
consequences, not causes: they arrive after the rail is already low.

**The power module and its connector to the FC have not been changed.** A new
flight controller does not fix a bad 5 V line. If that connector is carried
over to the X7+, expect this again.

## 4 — The camera's USB 3 link is what the GPS was hearing

The pilot reported the aircraft hovering clean with the Jetson powered down
and drifting with it running. Measured on the bench, aircraft still, pilot
flicking ch6 to mark each change:

```
STEP 1  Jetson + camera      sats 19   north sd 3.146   10s drift 1.694 m
STEP 2  camera unplugged     sats 22   north sd 1.733   10s drift 0.571 m
STEP 3  Jetson powered off   sats 23   north sd 1.104   10s drift 0.636 m
```

Unplugging the camera is most of it — three times less drift and three more
satellites with the Jetson still running. Powering the Jetson off after that
changes nothing more. **It is the camera's USB 3 link, not the Jetson board.**

GPS L1 is 1575.42 MHz, wavelength 19 cm, and the antenna sits 10-15 cm above
the camera. That is inside one wavelength, so the coupling is near-field and
cable quality does not settle it.

**HDOP read 0.61-0.64 through all three and never moved.** That is why the
first look at satellites and HDOP found nothing. The receiver reports a
healthy fix while its position wanders; only the scatter shows it. Do not use
HDOP to judge this.

### The shielding worked

The pilot shielded the cable. Measured the same way the next morning against
the previous night's eight-minute hover:

```
10 s drift    0.336 -> 0.193 m
east scatter  0.140 -> 0.092 m
satellites      19  -> 29
vibration     2.14 vs 2.19 m/s2 rms — unchanged, as it should be
```

Caveats kept honest: different time of day and different flight lengths. Both
together cannot account for three to four times.

`fc_scripts/emi_test.py` splits a bench log at the ch6 marks and scores each
part. Repeat it after any change to the antenna, the mast or the cable.

## 5 — The motors: a yaw the airframe never stops asking for

**Read the correction at the end of this section first: on the new board it
measures 59 PWM, not 235.**

```
frame ring (ArduPilot hexa-X test order 5-1-4-6-2-3)

   motor 5  front-right  1527.8      motor 1  right       1720.2
   motor 4  rear-right   1455.3      motor 6  rear-left   1728.6
   motor 2  left         1538.8      motor 3  front-left  1778.1
   {5,4,2}  mean 1507.3              {1,6,3}  mean 1742.3

   difference  235 PWM
```

Identical with the Jetson powered off, so nothing electrical touches it. The
aircraft hovers level and tracks attitude to 0.4 degrees, so lift is balanced
and all 235 PWM goes into holding a yaw the airframe keeps trying to turn.
Motors 3 and 4 are 323 PWM apart and sit opposite each other.

**This is mechanical and no parameter fixes it.** Capping `ATC_RAT_YAW_IMAX`
would only let the aircraft rotate instead of fighting, which is worse. Check,
in this order:

1. **Prop pitch and model** — are the three on `{1,6,3}` the same part as the
   three on `{5,4,2}`? A set replaced with a different brand produces exactly
   this, because the reaction torques no longer cancel at equal thrust.
2. **Motor mount angle** — any motor tilted tangentially turns thrust into yaw.
3. **Arm twist** — a carbon arm rotated on its own axis does the same.

### Correction, same evening: it is 59 PWM on the new board, not 235

Two flights on the X7+, measured from the FC's own RCOU while airborne:

```
                 {5,4,2}    {1,6,3}    difference
flight 364        1562.2     1620.7      58.6 PWM
flight 368        1561.2     1620.0      58.8 PWM
```

Per motor, flight 368: C1 1600, C2 1583, C3 1636, C4 1543, C5 1557, C6 1623.
Motors 3 and 4 are still the extremes, but 93 PWM apart rather than 323.

The two flights agree to 0.2 PWM, so this number is solid. The 235 above came
from a hover earlier the same day and I did not record which board or which log
it came from, so I cannot say whether the airframe changed or that measurement
was wrong. **Trust the 59.** It is still an imbalance worth chasing - props and
mount angle, motors 3 and 4 first - but it is nowhere near `MOT_YAW_HEADROOM`
any more, and it is not urgent.

`MOT_SPIN_ARM 0.140 -> 0.145` and `MOT_SPIN_MIN 0.150 -> 0.160`, measured
31 Oct with `motor_spin_cal.py`, props off: all six start at PWM 1145 and run
steadily at 1160. Both had come across from the quadcopter this flight
controller used to fly and had never been measured on 4008s with 16 inch
props. `build_restore_from_diff.py` leaves them alone now.

What the spin test did **not** record is which motor started last. That one
fact separates "motor or ESC difference" from "prop or mount". Re-run with
`--start 1100 --stop 1145` and watch which arm stays silent.

## 6 — GPS 2: Bad fix, and why it is not a bug

The pre-arm fails because the VPS port is configured (`GPS2_TYPE 14`) and
empty. It is empty because the node stops injecting entirely when the switch
asks for the M9N — deliberate, `live_node.py:3740`.

But switching to VPS does not fix it either. The node sends
`hdop = sigma / 1.5`, which at the 20 m an unmeasured origin declares is
**13.4**, against a `GPS_HDOP_GOOD` of **2.50** built for a satellite
receiver. `TODO.md:180` has had this written down for weeks.

The aircraft has flown 290 times with **`ARMING_SKIPCHK = 6156`**, which skips
compass (4), GPS lock (8), hardware safety switch (2048) and GPS config
(4096). The pilot chose to keep it. The honest alternative —
`GPS_HDOP_GOOD` raised to 15.0, which relaxes one threshold instead of
switching off four checks — was offered and not taken.

**On a new board, leave `ARMING_SKIPCHK` at 0 for the first arms.** Those are
exactly the checks that catch a bad calibration.

fixes and held 7.9 m; flight 286 took 29 and reached 36.6 m.

Stay within about 600 m of the origin. The matcher's window reaches 150 m and
the sheets are only 1.5-2 km tall.

## 8 — The `_use_pp` crash, fixed

`_use_pp` decided whether the gyro's rotation reached the trackers, and it was
assigned inside the `else` of the frame-age branch while the **second**
camera's call — outside that branch — read it anyway. A frame more than
`frame_age_max_s` stale left it unassigned and the process exited.

`vps_stderr.log` holds 109 crashes in three kinds and the last 25 are all this
one. It fires when the camera or the USB link falls behind, which happens
under machine load: on 29 Oct the node ran 65 minutes clean and then crashed
four times in eleven while a 50 MB sheet was written and read back.
`frame_age_s` reached 0.1135 against the 0.12 threshold in the run that died.

Fixed by hoisting `_gr`, `_gp` and `_use_pp` above the branch.
`tests/test_derot_flag_on_every_path.py` fails three ways on the build without
it. It has since proved itself live: a frame arrived at 0.1478 s and the node
carried on.

## 9 — What I got wrong, and how

Recorded because each one cost hours and the evidence that corrected it is
cheap to re-run.

**"The reset did not cost any tuning."** I saw `ATC_RAT_RLL_P` at 0.09 in the
snapshot and called it an ArduCopter default. The default is 0.135. 0.09 was
the tuned value and the aircraft was sitting on gains half again as high.

**A restore list chosen by hand.** My first list was 131 parameters I picked.
A full read found 62 still differing and about forty had never been in it —
the whole attitude tuning among them. Build from the measured diff
(`build_restore_from_diff.py`), never from judgement.

**Two exclusion patterns that were themselves wrong.** The calibration pattern
matched `^(INS_ACC|INS_GYR)` and swallowed `INS_ACCEL_FILTER` and
`INS_GYRO_FILTER` — filter cutoffs that feed the control loop, sitting at
20/20 against the 10/28 that flew. A first run would also have written
`BARO_GND_PRESS` and six RC calibration values over the fresh ones. Both were
caught before anything was written.

**"GPS_PRIMARY differed between the flights."** My diff kept each parameter's
**last** value. 286's went to 0 at 97.2 s because the pilot flipped the switch
at 96.2 s. Both flights started at 1. Always take the first value from a
dataflash PARM block.

**"The node never ran on 28 Oct."** I trusted directory timestamps while the
Jetson clock was 12 hours behind and the journal's boot record starts at 1970.

**"Log 14 is not a flight."** I measured airborne time from the barometer,
which was sitting half a metre below ground after the reset. It was a
482-second flight. Use the motors (`RCOU > 1200`), not the altitude.

**"The Jetson makes it better, not worse."** I compared an 84-second flight
where the pilot was working the sticks against a 482-second hover where they
were not. Normalised to equal stick-centred windows it is the other way round
by three to one. The pilot was right and said so twice before I measured it
properly.

**Motor grouping 1,3,5 against 2,4,6.** That mixes the two rotation
directions. The real ring is 5-1-4-6-2-3, the groups are `{5,4,2}` and
`{1,6,3}`, and the imbalance is 235 PWM, not the 101 I reported.

**"GPS2 msgs 0, so nothing went to the FC."** Incomplete. The `MAV` message
shows channel 2 carrying 2.8 packets/second from the Jetson. It is parameter
reads and heartbeats, it costs the FC nothing measurable (0 long loops,
`MaxT` 2532 vs 2542 µs), but the pilot's instinct that something was going
across was right.

**Tools that could not reach the FC.** `restore_params.py` connected and
listened without transmitting, so the router never registered it and it
reported "FC se heartbeat nahi aaya" with the FC plainly connected. A UDP
router only learns a client that sends. Fixed; `_announce` in `live_node.py`
has the same note.

### Flight 368 - the first flight that ever got a map fix

404 s airborne, 84.7 m, on `bam1.tif`. Switched to VPS at 41 m and back at
84 m, so **222 s on the VPS**.

The handover was clean - both frames re-anchor and the displacement zeroes,
exactly as designed. My first pass reported a 67 m excursion at the switch;
that was **one row**, the re-anchor sample, 67 m in the old frame and 0 in the
new. Not a real excursion. Real drift on the VPS leg: **p50 5.8 m, p90 14.1 m,
max 17.3 m**, growing from 1.68 m to 15.44 m across the 222 s.

**38 map fixes**, the first ever at this site.

```
score band    n   error vs M9N        altitude    n    error    score
0.05-0.08     6      56.3 m           60-70 m     2     2.7 m   0.185
0.08-0.11     7      22.1 m           70-80 m    25     5.9 m   0.131
0.11-0.14    15      31.9 m           80-90 m    11    53.6 m   0.079
0.14-0.25    10       5.0 m
```

Two things fall out of that, and both are new:

**The score knows when a fix is wrong.** corr(score, error) = **-0.606**. The
record until now said the matcher cannot tell; on this sheet it can. Raising
`min_score` from 0.05 to 0.12 would keep 18 of the 38 and drop the median error
from 27.0 m to 5.1 m. **That is a guard, so it was not applied** - it needs the
pilot's word.

**Above 80 m the matcher breaks.** 5.9 m at 70-80 m against 53.6 m at 80-90 m,
with the score falling 0.131 to 0.079 and the fix rate drying from 6 per 30 s
at 62 m to 1 at 84 m. So **60-80 m is the working band**, and a demo should fly
70-75 m, not 85.

The drift grew anyway because `max_correction_m = 0.5` - a fix can move the
estimate half a metre at most. That cap is what stopped the bad 80 m fixes
doing damage, and also what stopped the good ones helping.

Figures: `reports/flight_368/map_368.png`, `panels_368.png`.

```
t = 364.1   ERR GPS code 0        glitch cleared, 15 s after switching back
```

**The glitch was declared three seconds after the handover and did not clear
until fifteen seconds after the pilot switched back.** `EK3_GLITCH_RAD` is 25,
so the injected position was repeatedly more than 25 m from the EKF's own
prediction. Six EKF primary lane switches in between: the two cores disagreed
and ArduPilot kept changing its mind about which to believe. No failsafe fired
and the aircraft stayed in LOITER throughout - but none of this reaches the
node's log, which is why it was never known.

## 15 — Four operating notes found the hard way

**Never `systemctl restart drone-vps`.** The camera is left booted
(`03e7:f63b`) and the next process dies with `Failed to boot device!`, after
which systemd restarts it every five seconds for ever. Do this instead:

```
sudo systemctl stop drone-vps
# wait until lsusb shows 03e7:2485 (unbooted) - a second or two
sudo systemctl start drone-vps
```

**The RC switch's banner lies.** It prints `armed-switching=OFF (ground only)`,
but `gps_rc_switch.py:54` says `--allow-armed` is accepted and no longer read.
Switching while airborne works, and flight 368 did it twice. Do not let that
line talk a pilot out of switching in the air.

**The rangefinder is gone.** `RNGFND1_TYPE = 0` on the new board, and 0 of
13184 rows carried a reading. It was fitted on the old one.

**`sigma_m` under-declares.** On flight 364 the real gap exceeded the declared
sigma on 6405 of 10142 airborne rows - 63%. sigma p50 4.6 m against a real p50
of 5.97 m.

## 12 — Standing rules, unchanged

- Ask before writing or fitting any guard — anything that rejects, clamps,
  vetoes or zeroes.
- On the VPS, no GNSS data reaches the estimate. If the VPS stops the answer
  is ALT_HOLD, never an automatic fall back.
- Switch means switch. The pilot's flip is obeyed immediately.
- When asked a question, answer it. Do not start changing things.
- Commit and push to **both** branches after every change.
- Score position only against `gps1_north_m`/`gps1_east_m` — GPS_RAW_INT, the
  raw receiver — never `gps_north_m`, which is the EKF's fused answer and on
  the VPS is our own injection coming back.
- Filter `gps1_*` rows to within ~400 m of origin; they carry corrupt rows.
- Smooth both sides equally; score vectors as vectors, never magnitudes.
- Never take the declared accuracy from the receiver.
- The receiver is called the **M9N**. "GPS1/GPS2" means the FC's two ports.
